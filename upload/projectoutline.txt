# PROJECT: PROOFPATH
## A Premium Product-Journey, Skincare Wellness and Community Web Application

Build a complete, beautiful, functional, responsive web application called **ProofPath — Your Personal Product Journey Companion**.

This is a consumer-facing digital wellness application designed for customers of **Be Bodywise, a brand under Mosaic Wellness**.

The purpose of the application is to improve the customer experience after purchasing and beginning to use a skincare, hair-care, or body-care product. Customers often purchase products with certain expectations but may struggle to track their experience, understand product instructions, identify unwanted reactions, remember when their products will run out, or decide what to do next.

ProofPath brings these disconnected experiences together into one intelligent, personalised, aesthetically pleasing platform.

It combines product inventory management, progress tracking, AI-assisted skin support, replenishment prediction, product education, and a consent-based wellness community.

The application must feel like a premium digital extension of the Be Bodywise brand: trustworthy, modern, calming, elegant, supportive, and exceptionally easy to use.

**Do not build just a landing page or a static dashboard. Build a complete, interactive application with functional navigation, forms, product management, charts, reminders, a conversational AI interface, community interactions, and persistent data wherever the available environment supports it.**

# PART 2 — VISUAL DESIGN AND AESTHETIC DIRECTION

## 2.1 Overall creative direction

The application should look like a premium skincare and wellness product designed by an experienced product-design team.

Reference the visual quality of modern beauty brands, sophisticated editorial websites, minimalist lifestyle applications, and high-end wellness journals.

The design should be:

- Beautiful and visually cohesive.
- Calm, warm, and emotionally reassuring.
- Premium without feeling inaccessible.
- Feminine-leaning but inclusive.
- Modern and technology-driven without looking cold.
- Data-rich without feeling like a medical dashboard.
- Minimal, with generous whitespace and deliberate visual hierarchy.

Avoid generic AI-generated dashboard aesthetics, excessive gradients, oversized emoji, random illustrations, dense tables, and too many cards competing for attention.

Every screen should feel like it belongs to the same carefully designed product.

## 2.2 Colour palette

Use the following palette consistently:

- Warm ivory: #FAF8F3 — main application background.
- Pure white: #FFFFFF — cards and content surfaces.
- Soft sage: #DCE7D8 — progress, wellness, and positive accents.
- Deep forest green: #314C3C — primary buttons and important text.
- Muted lavender: #E9E2F2 — AI support and reflective content.
- Dusty rose: #E8C9C5 — gentle highlights.
- Warm charcoal: #343630 — primary body text.
- Muted grey: #777970 — secondary text and supporting information.
- Soft border: #E8E5DD — dividers and card outlines.

Use semantic colours consistently for warnings, errors, and success messages. Never rely on colour alone to communicate meaning.

The overall interface should be predominantly light, with forest green providing contrast and restrained lavender and sage accents creating a distinctive visual identity.

## 2.3 Typography

Use an editorial serif font such as DM Serif Display or Playfair Display for major headings.

Use Inter, Manrope, or a similar clean sans-serif font for body text, navigation, form fields, and data.

Headings should feel refined and editorial. Body text should be highly readable.

Use sentence case for most interface copy. Avoid unnecessary uppercase text.

## 2.4 Components and layout

Use:

- Rounded cards with 16–24px corner radii.
- Thin, understated borders.
- Soft, restrained shadows.
- Beautiful product imagery.
- Clean line icons, preferably Lucide.
- Progress rings and elegant line charts.
- Small status indicators.
- Spacious layouts with consistent spacing.
- Smooth but restrained transitions.
- Thoughtful empty states.
- Clear loading, success, error, and disabled states.

Use real whitespace to establish hierarchy rather than enclosing every item inside a separate card.

## 2.5 Responsive design

The app must work beautifully on:

- Desktop browsers.
- Laptops.
- Tablets.
- Mobile browsers.

On desktop, use a collapsible left sidebar and a spacious central content area.

On mobile, use a compact top bar and a bottom navigation bar.

Keep primary actions easy to reach. Ensure charts, modals, product forms, community posts, and AI chat remain usable on small screens.

## 2.6 Motion and micro-interactions

Add subtle animations for:

- Opening a product journey.
- Updating a progress chart.
- Completing a check-in.
- Adding a product.
- Updating remaining product quantity.
- Receiving an in-app reminder.

Use restrained motion and respect reduced-motion preferences.

Animations must never delay important actions or make the application feel slow.

# PART 6 — MY PRODUCTS: INVENTORY AND PRODUCT MANAGEMENT

This is one of the most important features.

Users should be able to add, view, edit, archive, and delete products.

## 6.1 Add Product

Create an elegant form titled:

“Add to My Products”

Include these fields:

- Product name.
- Brand.
- Product category.
- Product image.
- Product size or total volume.
- Unit of measurement.
- Purchase date.
- Opening date.
- Starting quantity.
- Estimated current quantity.
- Typical amount used per application.
- Frequency of use.
- Estimated applications per week.
- Optional product price.
- Optional purchase link.
- Optional expiry date.
- Optional batch number.
- Product notes.

Supported units should include mL, L, g, kg, pieces, capsules, tablets, and other appropriate units.

For products that cannot be measured precisely, provide manual quantity tracking.

Allow the user to mark a product as active, paused, finished, or archived.

## 6.2 Product detail page

Each product should have a dedicated page with:

- Product image and verified name.
- Quantity remaining.
- Original quantity.
- Estimated usage rate.
- Estimated days remaining.
- Projected run-out date.
- Purchase and opening dates.
- Associated product journey.
- Recent usage history.
- Upcoming reminders.
- Product information and instructions where available.

Use a visually attractive quantity indicator.

Example:

“Your product is approximately 65% used.”

“Estimated supply remaining: 18 days.”

If the remaining quantity is unknown, show an option to update it rather than fabricating a measurement.

## 6.3 Quantity tracking methods

Offer three methods.

**Method A: Manual update**

The user enters the current estimated quantity.

**Method B: Usage logging**

The user records the amount used during each application. If the application amount is known, the app adjusts the estimated remaining quantity.

**Method C: Quick visual update**

The user chooses:

- Full.
- About three-quarters.
- About half.
- About one-quarter.
- Almost empty.

These are user estimates, not automatic physical measurements.

## 6.4 Usage calculations

Implement real quantity calculations.

Formula:

Estimated daily consumption = Total estimated consumption over the tracking period ÷ Number of days in that period.

Estimated days remaining = Current estimated quantity ÷ Estimated daily consumption.

Estimated run-out date = Current date + Estimated days remaining.

Example:

- Starting quantity: 100 mL.
- Estimated current quantity: 30 mL.
- Estimated daily consumption: 2 mL.
- Estimated supply remaining: 15 days.

Display this calculation in an optional information panel so users understand where the estimate comes from.

Allow users to correct consumption assumptions.

Account for irregular usage and avoid showing misleading precision.

If consumption is zero, unknown, or insufficiently established, do not invent a run-out date.

# PART 7 — SMART REPLENISHMENT REMINDERS

This feature helps customers avoid running out of essential products unexpectedly.

## 7.1 Reminder scheduling

Provide customisable reminder settings for each product.

Default behaviour:

- First reminder: seven days before estimated depletion.
- Second reminder: three days before estimated depletion, if enabled.
- Final reminder: on the estimated run-out date, if enabled.

If a product is estimated to run out in 14 days, schedule the first reminder for approximately seven days later.

If the product is already expected to run out within seven days, show a low-stock alert immediately.

Recalculate reminders whenever the user changes the remaining quantity, usage frequency, or consumption estimate.

Avoid duplicate reminders.

When a product is marked finished or archived, cancel its pending replenishment reminders.

## 7.2 Reminder interface

Create a dedicated “Running Low” section.

Each reminder card should display:

- Product image.
- Product name.
- Estimated days remaining.
- Estimated run-out date.
- Reminder status.

Actions:

- View product.
- Update quantity.
- Reorder.
- Remind me later.
- Disable reminder.

If an official purchase link is available and verified, offer an external purchase link.

Never invent a product URL, place an order automatically, or claim that a purchase has occurred.

## 7.3 Notification delivery

Implement functional in-app reminders.

Use actual date-based scheduling logic rather than hard-coded countdowns.

If persistent reminders while the browser is closed are required, implement an appropriate backend scheduler or notification service when available.

Explain limitations if the generator cannot support background delivery.

Do not falsely claim that an in-app reminder has been delivered as a browser push notification or email.

# PART 9 — AI SKIN SUPPORT

Create a dedicated AI-assisted skin support feature called:

**“Ask Skin Guide”**

Supporting tagline:

“Tell us what you're experiencing. Let's work out a safe next step.”

This feature allows users to ask questions when they notice redness, itching, bumps, dryness, irritation, burning, or other changes after using a product.

It is a safety-conscious educational assistant, not a doctor or dermatologist.

Never label it “your AI doctor” in a way that suggests it is a licensed medical professional.

## 9.1 Chat interface

Create a beautiful, reassuring conversational interface.

Include:

- A welcome message.
- A visible explanation of limitations.
- Suggested question cards.
- Text input.
- Send button.
- Optional image upload, only when safely supported.
- Selected product context.
- Conversation history.
- Clear-history control.
- A prominent route to appropriate medical help.

Suggested prompts:

- “I noticed bumps after starting a new product.”
- “My skin feels itchy after applying a moisturiser.”
- “I have redness after using an exfoliating product.”
- “Help me understand this ingredient list.”
- “Help me prepare questions for a dermatologist.”

## 9.2 Information gathering

When a user describes a concern, the assistant should ask relevant, concise questions, such as:

- Which product did you use?
- When did you first notice the change?
- What symptoms are you experiencing?
- Are the symptoms mild, moderate, severe, or rapidly worsening?
- Is the reaction spreading?
- Have you stopped using the suspected product?

Do not overwhelm the user with a long questionnaire.

Prioritise urgent safety guidance if severe symptoms are already apparent.

## 9.3 Safe response behaviour

The AI must:

- Explain that timing alone cannot prove a product caused a reaction.
- Provide general educational information.
- Avoid definitive diagnoses.
- Avoid prescribing medication.
- Avoid unsupported claims about ingredients.
- Avoid recommending potentially irritating home remedies.
- Avoid advising the user to continue applying a product to test the reaction.
- Suggest appropriate professional evaluation when symptoms warrant it.
- Help users prepare a clear symptom summary for a qualified healthcare professional.

For suspected product irritation, the assistant may recommend avoiding further exposure to the suspected product pending appropriate advice.

Do not recommend introducing additional active skincare products to treat an unexplained reaction.

## 9.4 Emergency handling

If the user reports difficulty breathing, throat or tongue swelling, fainting, or other potentially severe allergic-reaction symptoms, immediately advise seeking emergency medical help.

For severe, rapidly worsening, widespread, painful, or blistering reactions, recommend urgent professional assessment.

For persistent or concerning bumps, recommend evaluation by a qualified healthcare professional.

Do not delay safety guidance in order to collect more information.

## 9.5 Product-aware guidance

When the user selects a product from My Products, allow the assistant to use its verified product information.

Where available, display:

- Product name.
- Ingredient list.
- Directions for use.
- Relevant precautions.
- Official product information link.

If product data is unavailable, ask the user to provide the label or consult the official product information.

Never invent ingredients or claim to know a product's composition without reliable data.

## 9.6 AI implementation

If a real AI service is available, connect it through a secure backend.

Store API credentials in server-side environment variables.

Use appropriate authentication, rate limiting, input validation, and error handling.

Use explicit safety instructions for health-related conversations.

Do not expose private conversations to the public community.

Do not use user health information for brand-facing analytics without separate, explicit consent.

If a live AI integration is unavailable, create a clearly labelled demonstration assistant with predetermined sample interactions. Do not pretend a simulated response was produced by a real medical professional or a functioning medical AI service.

# PART 16 — THE COMPLETE DEMO JOURNEY

The following sequence must be possible within the finished application.

### Step 1: Enter the app

The user opens ProofPath and sees the branded dashboard.

### Step 2: Add a product

The user adds a moisturiser with a starting volume of 100 mL.

They enter:

- Current estimated quantity: 30 mL.
- Estimated usage: 2 mL per day.

The app calculates approximately 15 days of supply remaining.

It displays the estimated run-out date and schedules the first reminder seven days before that date, subject to the user's preferences.

### Step 3: Create a journey

The user creates a skincare journey and records a baseline observation.

### Step 4: Log a check-in

The user records a weekly rating and an observation.

The app saves the entry and updates the timeline and chart.

### Step 5: Explore AI Skin Support

The user opens the AI assistant and selects the moisturiser.

They describe noticing bumps after applying it.

The assistant asks relevant questions, provides cautious educational guidance, avoids diagnosing the cause, and recommends professional evaluation when appropriate.

The user can save a private summary of their experience.

### Step 6: Explore the community

The user browses sample journeys, reads an experience, and sees the distinction between private and shared content.

The user can create a post and explicitly choose whether it is private or shared.

### Step 7: Return to the dashboard

The dashboard reflects the product, journey, check-in, and replenishment data created during the session.

The application should behave consistently throughout this sequence.
